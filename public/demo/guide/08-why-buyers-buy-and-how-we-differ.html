If a buyer remembers only one thing, it should be this.

The one-sentence case
LabelNest puts the people who raise capital, invest it, run programmes and research private markets on one consent-first network, built on structured data that every product reuses, so nobody types the same facts twice and nobody is shared without saying yes.

The three big differences

Difference 1

Consent-first data

Nothing about a founder reaches an investor, a funder or an outside party until the founder allows it. A new data room is listed by default and marked as raising, with no email sent, and one click switches it off. Contact is released only when the founder accepts. The consent record is append-only.

07 Objections and answers

24 objections grouped by buyer, each with the first sentence to say.

Fund managers

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
1. “We already have a CRM and spreadsheets.”A CRM is a good place for conversations. The gap is the portfolio: here it fills itself from each founder’s own data room, so you stop typing and chasing.
2. “Our founders will not fill in another tool.”They fill it in once for themselves, to raise and to apply, and your portfolio reads it. You are not asking them for a report.
3. “Can I trust AI-written memos?”Every AI output is a draft, and nothing is saved, sent or created until a person approves it.
4. “Do LPs get a login?”LPs get a password-protected shareable link, with LP reports, briefings and a questionnaire drafted only from your own documents.
5. “IC members will not create accounts.”They do not need to. Each member votes from a personal link, cannot see the others’ votes, and the archive keeps who voted what and why.

Angels, investors and family offices

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
6. “Why would founders list themselves?”Because it helps them be found and they stay in control: contact is hidden, they can switch off in one click, and an introduction needs their yes.
7. “Is this just another list of cold names?”No. Only real companies appear, with a sector, a stage or filled data room fields, ranked by fit with the reason shown.
8. “What does it cost me?”Nothing. Connect is free for investors.
9. “I do not want a chat or pooled money tool.”Co-invest is deliberately small: an invite by email, an NDA acknowledgement, and discovery and diligence only. No amounts, no chat, no money through LabelNest.

Founders

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
10. “I do not have time.”The first thing is a score that tells you the three things to fix this week, and you can import a spreadsheet or fill from your pitch deck, with suggestions you accept.
11. “Will it overwrite what I typed?”Never. Fill from your pitch deck shows current against suggested and writes only what you tick.
12. “I do not want to be listed.”Listing is one click off, and your contact stays hidden until you accept an interest request.
13. “Is it worth the money?”You start with a free 30-day pass. After that it is a 12-month plan at ₹24,999 plus GST, and you can pay upfront, pay later or opt out.

Incubators, universities and CSR programmes

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
14. “We run on spreadsheets and it works.”It works until a funder asks for outcomes or an auditor asks for approvals. Orbit keeps scores, mentors, finance and an uneditable audit trail in one place.
15. “Our team will not learn another tool.”The owner can preview Orbit as any staff role, as a mentor or as a founder before inviting anyone, so you test what each person will see.
16. “What do our founders get?”A pre-filled investor data room the moment they are selected, and no retyping of company details.
17. “Can funders see our founders’ private data?”Funders have a reports-only role. The admin can ask, but only the founder can allow, decline or withdraw, and the consent log is permanent.

Data buyers, banks and institutions

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
18. “We have a global data subscription.”Keep it where it is strong. Atlas is regional depth with linked records, sources and review dates, with a free tier so you can compare on your own question.
19. “How do I know the data is right?”Every researched field records its source, when it changed and when it was last reviewed. If a field is wrong, anyone can add a correction through Field Notes.
20. “Is it as big as the global names?”We sell depth in the regions we have covered, not a global replacement. Test it on a firm you know.
21. “Can we just buy a dataset?”Yes, on Exchange: buyers post a brief, verified sellers apply, and escrow-backed payment releases only when the work is approved.

Anyone

They sayFirst sentence, then the proof
22. “Why not just use separate best-of-breed tools?”You can. The cost is retyping the same facts into each one. Here the data room feeds the fund, the programme and the investor.
23. “Is Sentinel available?”Sentinel, for risk and AI governance, is next on the roadmap. It is a conversation, not something to buy today.
24. “Who sees our data?”Nobody outside, until the founder allows it. Permissions are checked by the server, not just hidden in the screen.
Rule
Answer the objection in one sentence, then show the screen that proves it. If the screen is not at hand, offer to show it in the next call rather than promising.
