01 The promise in one minute

Orbit is the incubation operating system inside NestLens. It replaces forms, spreadsheets and WhatsApp with one connected programme workspace.

The promise

Orbit takes a programme from application form to alumni in one place. Applicants fill a form you built. Evaluators score it. You shortlist, run rounds and select. The moment you select a startup, its investor data room on Ascent goes live with the application already inside. Mentors, events, grants, money and reports sit in the same system, so nobody re-types anything.

Application to alumniOne click to AscentRoles enforcedAudit-ready finance

Who buys it

The head of a programme, a director or a CEO.

IncubatorsAcceleratorsUniversity innovation cellsGovernment-backed centresCSR and corporate programmes

Especially strong where a funder wants to see every rupee: government schemes such as NIDHI, DST-TBI, BIRAC and RKVY-RAFTAR ask for exact records, filings and utilisation certificates.

The problem, in the buyer’s own words

📝

“Applications live in forms and sheets.”

Scores sit in different files. Nobody can say who scored what.

💬

“Mentors are on WhatsApp.”

Sessions are not recorded. Founders cannot rate help they received.

🧾

“Grants and utilisation are in Excel.”

An auditor asks for proof and the team scrambles.

The fourth problem
Founders re-type the same company details into every programme and then again into every investor tool. Orbit ends that: the application becomes the start of the founder’s data room.

03 The journey

From first application to alumni, in eight stages. Each stage has a screen.

Stage 1Set up

Create the workspace, invite the team, pick roles and branding.

Stage 2Open for applications

Build and publish the form, flip the switch, share the link.

Stage 3Score and shortlist

Evaluators score; Orbit averages; you shortlist and run rounds.

Stage 4Select and activate

Activate Data Room: the founder gets an invite and a pre-filled room.

Stage 5Run the cohort

Startup pages, mentors, sessions, events, courses, milestones.

Stage 6Money and compliance

Grants, disbursements, claims, approvals, filings.

Stage 7Capital and demo day

Capital Connect opportunities; investors ask to connect.

Stage 8Graduate and report

Briefings, analytics, alumni impact.

Programme in a year. Illustrative calendar from set-up to alumni reporting. (illustrative, in the product’s style)

January: the Director sets the stage

Creates the workspace, invites a Programme Manager, Finance, three Evaluators and an Auditor. Uploads the logo and picks colours. Sets an approval limit for money going out.

February to March: the form and the link

The Programme Manager builds the form with eligibility and five weighted criteria, adds a vetting check, writes the About page and presses Start accepting applications. The link goes on the website and social media.

March to April: scoring

Applications arrive. Each shows completion and an automatic partial score. Five evaluators score; AI drafts a note, a person edits it. The average appears on one screen.

April to May: shortlist and rounds

Round 1 advances the best. Round 2 uses the founder’s real Ascent readiness. Emails go out after a person reads them.

May: selection

The Director presses Activate Data Room on each selected startup. Each founder accepts an invite and lands in a room that already holds the application.

June to October: the cohort

Mentors are assigned and log sessions. Founders report milestones with evidence, post updates, take a course, RSVP to events. Finance disburses grants phase by phase. Claims and purchases follow the approval bands.

November: capital and demo day

Capital Connect opportunities go live. Investors on Connect ask to connect with listed founders; the requests appear on each startup page.

December: report and look ahead

Briefings prepare the Director for a funder meeting. Analytics and Compliance go to the funder. Graduates move to Alumni impact, and the next cohort starts from a template.

Aha moment
By May a founder who applied in March already has a working data room and an investor-ready profile. Nobody asked them to start over.
