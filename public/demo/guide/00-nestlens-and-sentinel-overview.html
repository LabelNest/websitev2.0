The problem

Raising money, running a fund and running an incubator all run on the same scattered things: spreadsheets, email threads, a data room link in one place, a pitch deck in another. The same company details are typed again for every programme, every investor and every report.

One connected network where a fund, its portfolio companies, other investors, incubators and the data layer reinforce each other. A company’s facts are built once, owned by the founder, and used wherever they are needed.

The words you will hear

Every term once, in plain English.

Data room
A structured record of a company (team, numbers, legal, documents) that an investor can review. In Ascent it is built once and reused.
Readiness score
A score out of 100 in Ascent showing how investor-ready a data room is, with the exact blockers.
Listed
A founder switch in Ascent: when on, matching investors on Connect can discover the company.
Thesis
What an investor says they back: sector, stage, geography, cheque size.
Fit score
How closely a company matches a thesis, shown as a percentage on Connect.
Cheque size
How much an investor writes into one company.
Consent
The founder’s permission. Nothing is shared or contacted without it.
NDA
A confidentiality acceptance that an investor ticks before opening an investor view.
Deal flow
The stream of startups an investor sees, with their status.
Pipeline
A fund’s deals in order, from first look to term sheet.
IC (investment committee)
The group that votes on a deal. In Command, members vote from a personal link.
Portfolio
The companies a fund has invested in.
NAV
Net asset value: what the fund’s holdings are worth today.
TVPI and DPI
Common fund return measures. TVPI is total value against money paid in; DPI is cash returned against money paid in.
Quarterly pack
Command’s report of fund performance with the source and month of every figure.
LP report
What a fund sends to its limited partners.
AIF
Alternative investment fund, the Indian regulated fund category.
Cohort
A batch of startups in an incubator programme.
Programme
One intake of an incubator, with its form, rounds and startups.
Activate Data Room
The Orbit button that opens an Ascent data room for a selected startup.
Kai
The NestLens AI assistant. It drafts; a person approves.
Atlas credits
What you spend in Atlas to open deeper data or run a Studio question. A free tier of 25 credits exists.
Exchange
The NestLens marketplace where structured datasets are bought and sold, with a masked sample first.
Provenance
The record of where a fact came from, when it changed and who reviewed it.
Intel Score
Atlas’s measure of how complete a record is.
Scout
An outside person who posts a thesis for a fund and reviews applicants.

Channel 3: the fundraise loop

This is the loop that drives growth on every track. Investors give startups a reason to come. Startups take Ascent because they are raising.

Step 1Investors publish a thesis

Angels, networks, VCs and family offices set up a free Connect profile: sector, stage, geography, cheque size.

Step 2Startups come to raise

Founders see who is investing in their space. To be discovered and to apply they need an investor-ready data room, which is Ascent. It starts with a free 30-day pass.

Step 3The fundraise happens

Investors scout matching startups and ask to connect. Founders accept or decline and control what they share.

Step 4Funds move to Command

A fund that backs a company sees the pipeline and portfolio in one place.

How the two sides feed each other

  • More theses give a startup a better reason to build a data room.
  • More startups with data rooms give investors a reason to stay active.
  • Investors are free, so the first investor meeting has no price barrier.
  • Startups take Ascent, so the loop earns revenue.

What you watch

  • Investors with a published thesis on Connect
  • Active investors scouting each month
  • Startups on Ascent that came to raise
  • Paid Ascent conversions from those startups
  • Introductions requested and accepted

07 How the platform earns trust

Six promises that sit under every product, and where you can see each one on screen.

🔒

Consent first

Nothing is shared with an investor or an outside party until the founder allows it. The founder decides who sees what, and can withdraw.

👤

Founder-controlled data

The founder builds the data room and owns it. Other products read it only with permission, and the founder can switch listing off in one click.

👁

Visible only with permission

Investors see an investor view only after accepting an NDA, and contact details only after the founder accepts. A room with nothing to evaluate stays hidden.

🛡

Derived scores only in Sentinel

Sentinel shows scores and reasons built with NestLens market intelligence. Customers never receive the underlying Atlas data.

🧾

Provenance behind Atlas

Every researched field records where it came from, when it changed and when it was last reviewed, and by whom.

🤖

AI drafts, people decide

Where AI writes, it produces a draft. It never saves, sends or creates anything until a person approves it.
